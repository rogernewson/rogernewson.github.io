                                                       PERSONAL PROFILE

 

I am an applied statistician and statistical programmer, with a wide range of research experience. This has been mainly medical, which is unsurprising, as I came to statistics from a genetics background. I work extensively on the analysis, management and validation of data. My analyses use a very broad range of methods, standard and non-standard, developing new methods when appropriate. As I have a broad, in-depth understanding of how statistical methods work (or otherwise), I find it easy to learn or develop new methods, and to gain a feel for their advantages and limitations. Although my skills are more technical than managerial, one of my strong points is communicating statistics to non-statistical people. I contribute to the writing of scientific reports and papers, sometimes as the corresponding author of a multi-authored multi-centre publication. I am also involved in teaching statistics to medical students and refereeing for medical and statistical periodicals.

 

                                                                    SKILLS

 

Statistical methods: Although not a career methodologist, I have published on (and use routinely in my day-to-day work) confidence-interval methods for rank and spline parameters that can be understood by non-statisticians, and which are not really “non-parametric”. I am also expert in using (and developing when necessary) scenario-comparison methods, propensity-score methods, and implementations of multiple-test procedures.

 

Statistical/numerical software: I have written and distributed over 120 Stata packages, mostly on the Statistical Software Components (SSC) website. I am also an experienced SAS programmer, have been a Genstat programmer, and have some experience of R, including integrating R with Stata.

 

Programming languages: Mata, C, SQL. Some knowledge of C++. Have previously been a FORTRAN programmer and a Pascal programmer.
